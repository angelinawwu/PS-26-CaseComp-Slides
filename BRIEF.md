# Brief

This is the team's research, gathered in one place so the slides can follow it.

## Problem statement

> It is difficult to foster real language learning that encompasses natural speaking, slang, and
> conversational skill while maintaining the engaging, flashy interface and user experience of
> Duolingo.

The slide version (the "problem" slide) is: *Real speaking skill — natural conversation, slang,
confidence — is hard to build without losing what makes Duolingo fun.*

## Solution: Duolingo Connect (Ansu's MVP)

*Real speaking practice, without the fear of talking to strangers.* The MVP is
`Duolingo-PS-2026-CaseComp/Ansu's MVP`, which runs live in the deck.

- **Tap Connect** and pick **Lily (AI)**, who is always available and offers low-pressure practice
  with real slang at natural speed. Or pick **a real learner**, matched with a learner or native
  speaker.
- **Your avatar speaks for you.** Your face is hidden by default.
- **Your voice, your call.** Use your real voice or a synthetic one (Bright, Warm or Deep), and
  switch anytime, even mid-call.
- **Duo Guardian** (safe by design): age-verified matching in separate adult, teen and kid pools;
  face hidden by default; a personal-info filter that mutes phone numbers, addresses and social
  handles in real time; and one-tap report and block.
- **During a call**, topic suggestions push local slang (for example, "me pones… porfa" instead of
  "quisiera"). Afterward, a recap shows time, turns and new phrases, with a rating and report
  button.
- **Courses**: Spanish, Japanese and Chinese, with partners Maya, Mateo, Diego, Kenji, Sora and Lin.

Ansu's framing of the problem: *"Scripted drills don't build conversational fluency, and learners
have no safe way to practice with real people. 18–24-year-olds are Duolingo's largest group (31%),
and the most likely to want live practice."* That stat needs a source before it goes on a slide.

The earlier MVP, **Character Chat** (the root of `Duolingo-PS-2026-CaseComp`), is a real two-device
WebRTC call with pitch-shifted Lily and Zari voices. It's useful as proof that the voice disguise
works on a real call.

## Pain points

- Duolingo tries to do too much. It should specialize in one area, or keep the original route
  available.
- Speech recognition doesn't pick up words well.
- It's mainly practice, with no teaching.
- It puts entertainment ahead of learning.
- There's no sign language.
- Users can follow minors (5-year-olds).
- Personal experiences:
  - Chinese (Elisa): tones.
  - Japanese (Ansu): TBD.
- Competitor: HelloTalk.

## What to keep (entertainment, Annabella)

- Immediate gratification and rewards for doing lessons.
- A very clear reward system, which motivates people to solve language literacy.
- The mascot.
- Getting people back after they lose a streak.

## Solution ideas on the table

- Connecting people through avatars, by call or text, possibly with Roblox-style voice chat.
- Using call data (with consent) to improve the overall experience and education.
- Only active users, past a certain level of experience.
- Segmenting by age and by skill score: an internal Elo, possibly displayed.
- Gamifying it.
